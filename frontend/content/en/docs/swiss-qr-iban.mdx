# QR-bills: pair the right account with the right reference

A **QR-bill does not always require a QR-IBAN**. The account type depends on the reference: a QR-IBAN with a QR reference, or an ordinary IBAN with SCOR or no reference. Recognising the format, resolving the bank and checking the QR text are three separate checks.

Documentation reviewed on **4 November 2026**. The examples below are fictitious contract examples, not payment instructions.

## Distinguish the combinations

| Creditor account | Reference type | Combination |
|---|---|---|
| CH/LI QR-IBAN | `QRR` | A QR reference is required. |
| Ordinary CH/LI IBAN | `SCOR` | An RF creditor reference under ISO 11649. |
| Ordinary CH/LI IBAN | `NON` | Empty reference field; a separate unstructured message may be present. |

A QR-IBAN cannot be paired with `SCOR` or `NON`. An ordinary IBAN cannot be paired with `QRR`. The [SIX QR-bill guidelines](https://www.six-group.com/dam/download/banking-services/standardization/qr-bill/ig-qr-bill-v2.4-en.pdf), § 4.3.2 and § 7.1, define these combinations.

For the version transition, SIX announces version 2.4 from 14 November 2026 and keeps version 2.3 valid until November 2027. Under version 2.4, QR-IBANs and QR references are for CHF; EUR uses an ordinary IBAN with SCOR or NON. See the [SIX versions page](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/banking-services/payment-standardization/standards/qr-bill.html). The checker’s transition behaviour is explained below.

## 1. Recognise the QR range without a bank lookup

For CH/LI, the IID occupies positions 5 to 9 of the compact IBAN. `30000–31999` is the QR range. The free formatting endpoint decomposes the number:

```bash
curl --fail-with-body -sS \
  'https://api.ibanforge.com/v1/iban/format?iban=CH4431999123000889012'
```

Excerpt from the formatting response, checked on 4 November 2026:

```json
{
  "iban": "CH4431999123000889012",
  "valid": true,
  "bban": {
    "bank_code": "31999",
    "account_number": "123000889012"
  },
  "upgrade_to_full_validation": "valid: true here means the IBAN is well formed (length, structure, mod-97), nothing more. POST /v1/iban/validate ($0.005, or keyless for the first 25 calls a week per source address) names the bank and its BIC with the source of that answer (where we hold bank-code data we may use), SEPA and VoP readiness, and, where it reads the national register (DE, AT, BE, SK, CZ, BG, CH, LI), whether the bank code is allocated at all. Real answers of that full validation, readable with a plain GET: https://api.ibanforge.com/v1/demo"
}
```

Read `bban.bank_code`, here `31999`. On this route, `valid: true` concerns the format and IBAN checksum. It does not prove allocation of the IID or existence of an account. Do not convert an ordinary IBAN into a QR-IBAN by replacing digits: obtain billing details from the creditor’s bank.

## 2. Resolve a QR-IID with its provenance

Set `IBANFORGE_API_KEY` to a [reusable API key](/docs/api-keys). The clearing route accepts a QR-IID directly:

```bash
curl --fail-with-body -sS https://api.ibanforge.com/v1/ch/clearing/30024 \
  -H "X-API-Key: ${IBANFORGE_API_KEY}"
```

The response can associate `qr_iid` with the institution’s ordinary `iid`. `is_qr_iid: true` identifies the QR row consulted. Also read `qr_iid_source`: `register` comes from register rows; `headquarters` denotes an inference from the head office. When multiple numbers are available, read `qr_iids`, not only the singular field.

`valid_on` dates the SIX row’s validity, not the last download. A row without an association keeps its own IID; no mapping is invented. `found: false` is a missing result in the served data, distinct from an HTTP error or payment rejection. The [clearing contract](/docs/ch-clearing) explains fields and redirects.

## 3. Read IBAN, reference and pairing results separately

Full validation can include a `clearing` block for CH/LI. Adding `reference` also returns `reference_check`:

```bash
curl --fail-with-body -sS https://api.ibanforge.com/v1/iban/validate \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -H "X-API-Key: ${IBANFORGE_API_KEY}" \
  --data '{"iban":"CH4431999123000889012","reference":"210000000003139471430009017"}'
```

This example uses a demonstration number in the QR range. An arithmetically correct reference and permitted pairing do not prove that an institution or account exists at that number.

| Result | Question answered |
|---|---|
| Top-level `valid` | Is the IBAN syntactically valid? |
| `reference_check.valid` | Does the reference pass its own check? |
| `reference_check.pairing` | Is the account-type and reference-type pairing permitted by the examined rule? |
| `bank_code_check`, `bic`, `clearing` | Which bank references were resolved, with which sources and limitations? |

`pairing` can be `ok`, `qrr_requires_qr_iban`, `scor_forbidden_with_qr_iban` or `not_applicable`. The last means this rule was not applied, never implicit approval. Even `pairing: "ok"` does not prove that the reference belongs to the expected invoice.

For a reference-only check, the free `GET /v1/reference/validate` service is described in the [OpenAPI contract](https://api.ibanforge.com/openapi.json). An indeterminate or unverifiable reference stays indeterminate; do not turn `valid: null` into a rejection.

## 4. Check Swiss QR Code text for free

`POST /v1/ch/qr-bill/check` takes the **decoded text** in `payload`, not a photo or PDF. Preserve line breaks and empty fields: their positions are part of the format.

```bash
curl --fail-with-body -sS https://api.ibanforge.com/v1/ch/qr-bill/check \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  --data-binary @- <<'JSON'
{"payload": "SPC\n0200\n1\nCH4431999123000889012\nS\nSociété Alpha\nRue Exemple\n1\n1003\nLausanne\nCH\n\n\n\n\n\n\n\n10.00\nCHF\n\n\n\n\n\n\n\nQRR\n210000000003139471430009017\nExemple fictif\nEPD"}
JSON
```

Read the HTTP status, then `valid` and every `findings` entry. A missing or malformed request body returns `400`; analysed QR text can return `200` with `valid: false` and detailed errors. The HTTP status alone does not validate the bill.

- `creditor_iban` and `reference` describe the number and reference checks.
- `creditor` and `ultimate_debtor` describe addresses; a `proposed_structured` suggestion requires review before reissuing the bill.
- `findings` includes each finding’s code, severity and source. Warnings do not automatically make `valid` false.
- `ready_for_2026_11_14` means the check is valid and present addresses are structured. Its historical name creates neither a universal deadline nor an acceptance guarantee.

The checker applies version 2.3 and reports version 2.4’s EUR restriction with the warning `qr_iban_chf_only_ig24`. An EUR case with a QR-IBAN can therefore remain `valid: true` while carrying this warning. Read findings and the intended version, not only the readiness boolean.

The free check does not consult the bank account or verify the printed document’s layout. Locality findings do not validate the street. The [QR-bill tool](/tools/qr-bill) uses the same check; address rules are explained [separately](/docs/structured-addresses).

A public example: the [Geneva cantonal tax administration](https://www.ge.ch/paiement-impots) states that the QR code on its QR-bills issued before 12 March 2026 does not respect the separation of address fields, and that the data on those bills stays valid and can be entered by hand. When the text of a QR code carries a type K address, the check returns the finding and the `proposed_structured` block; it does not say what the bank will decide.

## Combined addresses: notice shared by the checks

Address type K has not been permitted in a QR-bill since 21.11.2025 (SIX, QR-bill IG 2.3). The banks guarantee payment of such a QR-bill only until the end of September 2026 (UBS: 30.09.2026; ZKB: end of September 2026 at the latest). A type K QR-bill may therefore be refused at payment: reissue it with type S. From 14.11.2026, the Swiss Payment Standards require a structured or hybrid address in a payment order, with the town and country in their own fields. The SIC system does not validate addresses: each bank decides. Some still accept domestic Swiss payments for now; a SEPA or international payment without a town may be rejected. Also redo the standing orders and templates captured from a type K QR-bill.

[SIX](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/banking-services/payment-standardization/swiss-payments.html) · [UBS](https://www.ubs.com/ch/de/services/payments/connection-ubs/iso-20022/structured-addresses.html) · [ZKB](https://www.zkb.ch/de/lps/unternehmen/zahlen/neuer-zahlungsverkehr.html)

## For a billing integration

Keep the original text and findings, have unknown combinations or sources reviewed, and obtain correct details from the creditor. None of these results confirms the account holder, replaces a payee check or authorises a payment. A QR-IBAN is not an indicator of a virtual account; [issuer classification](/docs/compliance) has a separate scope.
