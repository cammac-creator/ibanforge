There is no single worldwide date for the end of unstructured payment addresses. The EPC’s decision on 9 September 2026 supersedes the earlier statement that its 15 November deadline still stood. The Swiss timetable must be read separately.

> **Checked on 10 September 2026.** The [EPC decision of 9 September](https://www.europeanpaymentscouncil.eu/news-insights/news/epc-delays-address-format-migration-timeline) maintains unstructured addresses beyond 15 November 2026 under all five payment schemes; the new end date is to be set in October. [Swift’s announcement of 27 August](https://www.swift.com/news-events/news/swift-accepts-community-request-extend-structured-address-migration-iso-20022-payment-messages) defers the payments changes without a replacement address date, with an update by December at the latest. [SIX](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/banking-services/payment-standardization/swiss-payments.html) still publishes its separate Swiss timetable.

The last three rows retain the date of their earlier source review. Confirm those operators’ latest announcements before fixing a migration date; an EPC or Swift postponement is not an automatic change to another rail.

## Keep the historical dates separate

**22 November 2026 was an earlier EPC deadline.** The EPC’s guidance version 2.1 changed it to 15 November 2026. The September 2026 decision now postpones that later date too. Neither should be presented as the current EPC end date.

**October 2025 and November 2025 concern earlier changes.** EPC guidance allows hybrid addresses from 5 October 2025. SIX introduced hybrid addresses in Swiss payment orders in November 2025; its QR-bill standard separately removed combined type K addresses. A QR-bill’s address rules and a payment order’s address rules are not interchangeable.

## What this means if you issue Swiss payment files

SIX still publishes 14 November 2026 for the address requirements applicable to Swiss payment orders when an address is used. Prepare supplier data in a format supported by the relevant payment rail, then check your bank’s requirements. A delay announced by another operator does not by itself change this timetable.

For QR-bills the question is already settled: the combined address (type K) left the standard in November 2025, and only the structured type S remains.

Three things on this site help, free of charge: [`POST /v1/address/check`](/docs/structured-addresses) tells you whether an address you have structured passes the Swiss, HVPS+ or Fedwire rules, each finding naming its document; [`POST /v1/ch/qr-bill/check`](/tools/qr-bill) reads a whole QR-bill and says whether its addresses are type S or still type K, with the structured form proposed; and the [creditor file audit](/audit) runs both over an entire creditor file.
