An ERP import, supplier-data migration or upcoming payment run is an opportunity to review bank details. The useful output is a traceable list of findings: what to ask the supplier, what to compare with a register and what to leave to the bank. A file check alone cannot guarantee payment execution.

**Calendar reviewed on 4 November 2026.** SIX retains 14 November for SPS 2026; SIC does not validate addresses and banks may offer options outside SPS. The EPC will decide its new deadline by February 2027: that month is not a cutover date. See the [dated decisions by payment route](/blog/2026-09-02-which-date-structured-addresses) and the [SIX publication](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/banking-services/payment-standardization/swiss-payments.html).

## 1. Identify columns without changing values

Work from an export and keep the original. Keep IBANs as text; internal references let you locate the record in the ERP. Check the detected columns before interpreting the report: IBAN, BIC, name, street, building number, postcode, town and country where present.

A missing column limits the check. Without address columns, for example, the audit has not checked the address format. A supplier name in the file is not a name confirmed by the bank.

## 2. Check the IBAN structure

Length, country format and the mod-97 checksum identify input inconsistencies. National checks may supplement the result, depending on the country and available references. Passing these checks does not establish that an account exists or identify its holder. [Read the IBAN response fields](/docs/iban-validate).

For a Swiss QR-bill, check the IBAN/reference pairing separately. The [QR-bill checker](/tools/qr-bill) examines the complete code; uploading an IBAN column to the file audit is not a complete QR-bill check.

## 3. Read the bank-register finding

A bank reference can confirm the institution associated with a code. An absent code is classified as unallocated only when the consulted register supports that conclusion. A partial, unavailable or absent register does not provide the same evidence.

Keep the source and its date. If a code is marked as retired, review the published information and ask for confirmation of the bank details. Do not automatically replace the IBAN or BIC with a successor’s details. [Reference coverage and limits](/docs/data-sources).

## 4. Compare an existing BIC without overwriting it

The audit compares the first eight characters of the supplied BIC with those of the reference BIC when both are available. A difference calls for review; agreement does not identify the account holder or settle every routing question.

Keep the original value separate from any proposal. Whether a BIC is needed depends on the payment and the bank’s instructions. An empty field is not a reason to invent one.

## 5. Review duplicates and country differences

The same IBAN can appear in several records. This may reflect duplicate data or an arrangement that needs explaining; the audit does not decide which records to merge.

Likewise, a supplier based in Switzerland may use an account in another country. A difference between address country and IBAN country is a review signal, **not proof of an error or fraud**. Do not change the address country to match the IBAN.

## 6. Prepare addresses for the payment route

The required format depends on the standard and message. Under SPS, when an address is supplied, town and country must be populated separately. The [address-check documentation](/docs/structured-addresses) states each rule’s scope and source.

Splitting a free-text address requires reviewing its content. Do not infer a town or country from the IBAN. Confirm the format accepted for the relevant flow with your bank and software provider; a format finding does not predict their execution decision.

## 7. Keep payee verification in the banking process

The file contains the name you entered. IBANforge does not query the account holder. The EPC’s [Verification of Payee service](https://www.europeanpaymentscouncil.eu/what-we-do/other-schemes/verification-payee-vop) conveys a comparison through payment service providers; possible outcomes include a match, close match, no match or an unavailable check.

Maintain your procedure for confirming changes to bank details. A row with no audit finding replaces neither that procedure nor your bank’s result.
